# Implementation Project Insights

## What Makes Implementation Projects Different

You're not building from scratch — you're deploying something that exists into an environment with existing people, processes, and systems. Success is adoption, not just installation.

## Real-World Wisdom for Implementation Projects

### Planning Phase

- **Underestimating change management** - The technology is the easy part. Getting people to use it is hard.
- **"We'll train them later"** - Training is never an afterthought. It needs as much planning as configuration.
- **Data migration is always harder than expected** - Data is dirty 100% of the time. Plan 2-3x what you think you need.

### Vendor & Product Realities

- **COTS products have limits** - Commercial off-the-shelf software never does exactly what you want. Customize carefully.
- **Customization creates upgrade debt** - Every customization makes future upgrades harder. Default to configuration.
- **Vendor estimates are optimistic** - They want the sale. Add 30% to what they quote.
- **Proof of concepts lie** - POCs use idealized data and scenarios. Plan for reality.

### Rollout Wisdom

- **Pilot with influencers, not just volunteers** - The people who sign up first aren't always the people who influence adoption.
- **Go-live is the beginning, not the end** - Hypercare support is critical. Plan for 2-4 weeks of intensive support.
- **Parallel running is expensive** - Running old and new systems together requires real work. Plan for it.
- **User adoption curves** - Expect productivity to dip before it improves. Set expectations.

### Organizational Realities

- **Process change is political** - Changing how people work threatens someone. Find resistors early.
- **Train the trainers** - Scaling training requires multipliers. Identify champions.
- **Integration is where projects die** - System-to-system interfaces are underestimated 90% of the time.

### Common Failure Patterns

- Treating implementation as just a technical exercise
- Skipping pilot because "we're behind schedule"
- No rollback plan when going live
- Assuming the vendor will handle everything
- Underbudgeting for hypercare and ongoing support
- Ignoring the people who have to change their daily work
