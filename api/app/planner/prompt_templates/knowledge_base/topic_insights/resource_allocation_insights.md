# Resource Allocation Topic Insights

## Real-World Experience

### Do's
- Know who is actually available vs. named on a plan - "assigned" doesn't mean "available"
- Identify skill gaps early so training or hiring can happen - skills don't appear overnight
- Plan for ramping time - new people aren't productive on day one
- Build relationships with shared resource managers - negotiating access requires goodwill
- Track actual utilization against plan - you'll learn valuable patterns

### Don'ts
- Don't assume people are 100% dedicated to your project - everyone has other responsibilities
- Don't put key knowledge in one person's head (bus factor of 1) - spread critical skills
- Don't overload your best people - they become bottlenecks and flight risks
- Don't ignore tool and infrastructure needs - they're resources too

### Warnings
- Resource plans often assume ideal availability - real availability is 60-70% of that
- Part-time resources often deliver less than their percentage implies - context switching has costs
- Vacation, sick days, and attrition are guaranteed - plan for them
- Key person risk is real - what happens if your architect leaves?

### Recovery Tips
- If understaffed, prioritize ruthlessly - better to do fewer things well than many things poorly
- When key resources leave, immediately assess: What knowledge walked out the door? Can we document it from others?
