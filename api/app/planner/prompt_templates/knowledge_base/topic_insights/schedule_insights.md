# Schedule Topic Insights

## Real-World Experience

### Do's
- Build in 20-30% buffer for unknowns - there are always unknowns
- Map the critical path and protect it - one late activity here delays the whole project
- Break work into chunks smaller than 2 weeks - longer tasks hide complexity and delay feedback
- Plan for 70% productivity - meetings, context-switching, and surprises consume the rest
- Get buy-in on the schedule from people who have to deliver it - imposed schedules breed resentment

### Don'ts
- Don't work backward from a deadline without reality-checking - physics doesn't care about wishes
- Don't assume parallel work is free - coordination overhead eats time
- Don't forget deployment, testing, and stabilization phases - they're never as fast as hoped
- Don't schedule key people at 100% - they have other work, meetings, and unplanned items

### Warnings
- Schedules without buffers are plans to be late
- The "last 10%" of most projects takes 30% of the time
- When everything is on the critical path, your schedule is fragile - look for ways to decouple
- Schedule pressure often leads to shortcuts that create technical debt

### Recovery Tips
- If slipping, first look for scope cuts - descoping is usually faster than crashing
- Add resources carefully late in projects - onboarding overhead often outweighs benefit (Brooks's Law)
