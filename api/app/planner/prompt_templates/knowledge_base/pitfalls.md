# Common PM Pitfalls

Mistakes that experienced project managers have learned to avoid the hard way. These patterns consistently lead to project failures.

## The Planning Traps

- **The "Yes" Trap** - Agreeing to every request without assessing impact. Every "yes" costs something. Learn to say "yes, and here's what that means for timeline/budget."

- **The Optimism Trap** - Believing the best-case scenario. Hope is not a strategy. Plan for what will go wrong, not what might go right.

- **The Deadline Trap** - Working backward from a deadline without reality-checking. Just because the VP wants it by Q1 doesn't mean physics allows it.

- **The "Almost Done" Trap** - Tasks that are 80% done for weeks. The last 20% often takes 80% of the time. Probe deeply when you hear "almost done."

## The Communication Failures

- **Assuming silence is agreement** - If stakeholders don't respond to an email, they may not have read it. No news is not good news.

- **Communicating once and moving on** - Important messages need to be repeated 7 times through multiple channels before they stick. Don't be afraid to repeat yourself.

- **Saving bad news** - Hiding problems hoping they'll resolve themselves. They won't. Bad news only gets worse with age.

- **Over-relying on email for complex topics** - Some things need a conversation. If an email needs more than 3 paragraphs, schedule a call instead.

## The Scope Disasters

- **Scope creep disguised as "small changes"** - "Just one more field" becomes 20 fields. Every small change has hidden complexity.

- **Not documenting what's out of scope** - People assume features that aren't explicitly excluded. Write down what you WON'T do.

- **Gold plating** - Adding features no one asked for because "they might like it." They won't thank you – they'll ask why you're late.

- **Moving goalposts without resetting expectations** - When scope doubles, timeline stays the same. The math doesn't work.

## The Team Mistakes

- **Assuming "I told them" means "they understood"** - Communication isn't complete until it's confirmed. Always verify understanding.

- **Solving problems for people** - Instead of teaching them to solve problems themselves. This creates dependency and doesn't scale.

- **Ignoring the quiet team members** - The loudest voices don't always have the best ideas. Actively solicit input from introverts.

- **Loading key people at 100%+ capacity** - They become single points of failure. When they're sick or leave, everything stops.

## The Process Failures

- **Skipping kickoffs "because everyone knows the project"** - They don't. People have different mental models. Align explicitly.

- **No change control process** - Changes happen organically and no one tracks impact. You can't manage what you don't measure.

- **Status meetings that never discuss risks** - Everything is "green" until it's suddenly red. Force risk conversations weekly.

- **Post-mortems that assign blame** - People stop being honest. Focus on "what can we do differently" not "who messed up."

## The Antipatterns

- **Hero Culture** - Rewarding heroics means you get more crises that require heroics. Reward prevention, not firefighting.

- **Analysis Paralysis** - Waiting for perfect information that never comes. Make decisions with 70% confidence and adjust.

- **The HIPPO Effect** - Highest Paid Person's Opinion wins every argument. Truth should win, not seniority.

- **Invisible Progress** - Working for weeks with nothing to show. Break work into daily demonstrable progress.
