# Rollout Phase Topic

## PMI Definition
Rollout phases define the staged approach to deploying a solution into the operational environment. A phased rollout reduces risk by piloting with limited users before full deployment, allowing for learning and adjustment.

## What to Understand
- Rollout strategy (big bang vs phased)
- Pilot group and criteria
- Phase sequence and timing
- Success criteria for each phase
- Cutover and go-live planning

## What to Infer
From scope and stakeholders, infer:
- Natural user groups for phasing
- Geographic or functional phasing
- Risk-based phasing decisions
- Training dependencies

## What to Probe (Only if Missing)
- "Will this go live all at once or in phases?"
- "Who would be good candidates for a pilot?"
- "What would make you confident to expand to the next phase?"

## Intelligent Behaviors
- Recommend phased approach for complex rollouts
- Flag go-live risks
- Suggest pilot success criteria
- Connect rollout to change management
- Identify training dependencies

## Common Pitfalls to Flag
- No pilot before full rollout
- Missing rollback plan
- Inadequate training before go-live
- No hypercare support planned
- Success criteria not defined

## Rollout Strategies
| Strategy | When to Use |
|----------|-------------|
| Big Bang | Low risk, urgent timeline, simple change |
| Phased | High risk, complex change, learning needed |
| Parallel | Critical systems, need fallback |
| Pilot | New technology, uncertain adoption |

## Rollout Phase Components
| Phase | Activities |
|-------|------------|
| Pilot | Limited users, collect feedback, validate |
| Wave 1 | Early adopters, refine based on pilot |
| Wave 2+ | Broader rollout, scaled support |
| Hypercare | Intensive support post-go-live |
| Steady State | Normal operations and support |
